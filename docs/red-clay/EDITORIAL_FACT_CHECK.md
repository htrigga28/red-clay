# Red Clay Coffee editorial fact check

Date accessed: 2026-08-28

## Scope

This file supports the revised Origins pages, Volume 01 of The Editions, and the short regional claims on Home and About.

The source list favors government records, research institutions, peer-reviewed research, and recognized coffee-sector organizations. The approved product names, process labels, tasting notes, and stated formats come from `PRODUCT_CONTENT_CANON.md`. They are not research claims.

The customer copy must keep these limits:

- A region does not guarantee a flavor.
- A process name does not guarantee quality or flavor.
- A variety name does not predict the cup by itself.
- Evidence from one washing station does not describe every station in a region.
- The Hawassa photographs show work near Hawassa. They do not prove that the pictured coffee came from Sidama or Guji.

### Home and About wording: Central Kenya

Use: **“Central Kenya’s cultivated highlands and washed-coffee traditions.”**

This wording is concise and supportable. Keep “traditions” plural. Do not write “the home of washed coffee,” “always washed,” or a fixed flavor claim. If more space is available, use: **“Central Kenya’s cultivated coffee highlands and long association with washed processing.”**

### Home and About wording: Kayanza

Narrow the proposed phrase **“Kayanza’s steep hills and raised drying beds.”** It can imply that every Kayanza station uses the same equipment.

Use one of these versions:

- **“Kayanza’s steep coffee-growing hills and the raised beds used at documented washing stations.”**
- **“Kayanza’s steep coffee-growing hills, shared stations, and careful drying.”**

The second version is shorter and has the stronger evidence base.

### Home and About wording: Southern Ethiopia

Use: **“Southern Ethiopia’s exceptional coffee diversity.”**

This is supportable when nearby copy explains the context. For a stand-alone factual sentence, use: **“Southern Ethiopia holds important diversity within *Coffea arabica*.”** The narrow form avoids the false idea that every farm or retail lot has the same level or type of diversity.
