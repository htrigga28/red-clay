# Red Clay Coffee — Visual Reference Atlas

**Document version:** 1.1.0  
**Change note:** Round 1.1 promotes Canyon's collection, PDP, and navigation patterns from general inspiration to approved implementation baselines while preserving Red Clay transformation and anti-copy limits.  
**Reference rule:** These screenshots are evidence for composition and interaction patterns, not layouts to reproduce. Canyon is primary; Onyx is secondary. Screenshot filenames are stable evidence labels until a final asset library is established.

## Reference translation principles

- Borrow systems, not surface identity.
- Canyon contributes restraint, whitespace, image dominance, hierarchy, and commerce/editorial pacing.
- Red Clay transforms those systems through laterite, basalt, bone, raw clay, material studies, regional authorship, and stronger but bounded motion.
- Onyx contributes confidence and occasional promotion-scale composition; reject its density, promo-wall feeling, and futuristic tendencies.
- Essential information and actions cannot depend on hover.

## Canyon — collection and product discovery

### REF-CANYON-COLLECTION-OPEN-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122120(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Canyon online-shop opening and first product row.  
**Observe:** Very large whitespace between nav and title; thin, quiet header; large serif collection title; four image-dominant products; metadata sits below imagery with little chrome; no filter rail.  
**Why it works:** The collection feels curated rather than crowded, and the images do the merchandising. The quiet intro makes a small catalog feel intentional.  
**Borrow conceptually:** No-filter opening, editorial top spacing, image-first product anatomy, compact metadata, grid aligned to the page frame.  
**Red Clay change:** Use Canyon's calm image-led collection grid as the desktop baseline. Keep the four coffees dominant; let the Kiln Cup occupy a secondary row, different span, or distinct visual scale. Add region/process/notes with restraint and introduce bone/basalt/laterite material identity.  
**Must not copy:** Canyon wordmark proportions, exact four-column dimensions, pale pack design, title wording, or California softness.  
**Informs:** `/shop` intro, `COMP-PROD-CARD`, Homepage Current Harvest.  
**Viewport:** Desktop.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY.

### REF-CANYON-CARD-HOVER-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122128(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Same shop grid with one product hovered.  
**Observe:** Product image swaps to a contextual/lifestyle secondary image; a dark, narrow `Add to Cart` bar appears at the bottom of the image; the card itself does not lift, scale, bounce, or gain a rounded shell.  
**Why it works:** Interaction adds purchase utility and product context without disturbing the grid.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Alternate-image swap and restrained quick-add reveal within the media area; static card geometry; fast recovery on pointer leave.  
**Red Clay change:** Use detail, ritual, opened-pack, or material secondary imagery; quick-add may open a format sheet when selection is required; retain visible title/price outside the hover layer.  
**Must not copy:** Exact black bar dimensions, wording, product imagery, or hover timing. Do not hide grind/format requirements.  
**Informs:** Product card hover, quick-add, related-product strips.  
**Viewport:** Desktop; mobile needs an always-visible or explicit touch action.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY.

### REF-CANYON-COLLECTION-GRID-02

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122202(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Canyon “All Coffee” collection with denser product inventory.  
**Observe:** Oversized serif heading acts as a category statement; consistent neutral image fields allow varied packaging colors; four-column merchandising continues with generous row spacing; controls remain nearly absent.  
**Why it works:** A larger collection remains calm because the visual system is consistent and metadata is brief.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Clear collection statement, consistent image ground, disciplined row spacing, no filter UI when catalog size does not justify it.  
**Red Clay change:** Keep the clean grid and generous row spacing even with five items; use deliberate non-uniformity only to distinguish the Kiln Cup. Region and sensory information should be more meaningful than category labels.  
**Must not copy:** Equal-sized infinite grid, packaging palette, heading sentence, or Canyon’s category taxonomy.  
**Informs:** `/shop`, future catalog scalability, image background consistency.  
**Viewport:** Desktop.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY.

## Canyon — navigation

### REF-CANYON-NAV-MEGA-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122225(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Expanded Canyon Shop mega-menu over a dimmed shop page.  
**Observe:** Menu expands from the header into a white editorial field; links are grouped into short columns; one contextual lifestyle feature sits at right; background content is dimmed; navigation remains typographic and spacious.  
**Why it works:** It connects discovery and editorial merchandising without adding boxed navigation cards.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Calm overlay, grouped hierarchy, optional single feature image, strong backdrop separation, visible bag/search utility.  
**Red Clay change:** Prototype a restrained editorial reveal menu. Shop may expose the four coffee lots, Kiln Cup, and one current-harvest/Edition feature; Origins may expose the three regions and one contextual image. Reduce to the simpler header if browser testing makes the catalog feel inflated. Mobile keeps simple primary links.  
**Must not copy:** Mega-menu scope, column labels, dimensions, or persistent merchandising panel at launch.  
**Informs:** Current desktop header/menu prototype; mobile menu restraint.  
**Viewport:** Desktop.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY prototype direction; keep only after browser validation.

## Canyon — PDP hierarchy and continuation

### REF-CANYON-PDP-TOP-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122427(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Beachwood coffee PDP top.  
**Observe:** Three-part desktop composition: concise purchasing copy/actions on left, dominant pack image in center, pale metadata/spec panel on right; generous whitespace; section navigation beneath; first editorial headline begins immediately after the commerce composition.  
**Why it works:** Buying and product character are understandable at a glance, and editorial depth follows without becoming a separate experience.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Immediate buying decision, dominant image, compact metadata, bounded sticky potential, visible bridge into editorial continuation.  
**Red Clay change:** Use a three-zone hierarchy as the primary desktop prototype: purchase/identity/tasting/options, dominant product imagery, then restrained origin/process/dispatch metadata. Zones need not be equal. The earlier 58/42 layout is an alternate browser prototype, not the default. After utility, expand into publication-scale sensory, image, place/process, Edition, and related-harvest chapters.  
**Must not copy:** Exact column ratios, pale green panel, typography, tabs, or product facts.  
**Informs:** Coffee PDP hero/purchase, Kiln Cup PDP top, metadata block.  
**Viewport:** Desktop.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY.

### REF-CANYON-PDP-DETAIL-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122457(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** PDP editorial/sensory continuation and customer-review area.  
**Observe:** An oversized serif sensory statement spans the page; small information blocks and review columns sit below with extreme whitespace and fine rules.  
**Why it works:** One strong sentence becomes a visual chapter marker, while secondary information stays subordinate.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Large editorial statement as transition, quiet metadata/review-scale typography, controlled rules, uneven vertical rhythm.  
**Red Clay change:** Replace reviews/star ratings with verified origin/process/sensory context or factual reference material; Red Clay explicitly avoids ratings. Use place-driven copy and material/origin imagery.  
**Must not copy:** Star ratings, testimonial layout, precise statement, or whitespace proportions without content testing.  
**Informs:** Coffee PDP product-character transition and dossier statement moments.  
**Viewport:** Desktop.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY for hierarchy; prohibited content pattern for ratings.

### REF-CANYON-PDP-NARRATIVE-02

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122555(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Longer PDP brand/place narrative followed by reviews.  
**Observe:** A single wide serif paragraph is given page-level prominence; the reading experience remains integrated into the PDP; small content below does not compete.  
**Why it works:** It proves a PDP can carry editorial identity without a stack of boxed modules.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Full-width typographic chapter, long-form commerce continuity, deliberate change in content density.  
**Red Clay change:** Use a narrower readable measure for multi-sentence body copy, pair with a region/process plate, and link to the dossier/Edition rather than repeating the full story.  
**Must not copy:** Canyon location story, full-width line length for long copy, or customer-review system.  
**Informs:** Coffee PDP Place & Story; About; Edition commerce bridges.  
**Viewport:** Desktop.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY.

### REF-CANYON-RELATED-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122630(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** “You Might Also Like” row and “Perfect Pairings” lifestyle modules.  
**Observe:** Related products use the same calm product-card language; the next chapter shifts from pack images into broader lifestyle/editorial plates; labels and view-all actions are minimal.  
**Why it works:** Cross-sell feels like continued curation rather than an aggressive upsell carousel.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Related-product continuity, one clear section label, restrained merchandising, transition from product to ritual/context.  
**Red Clay change:** Recommend at most three relevant coffees or one Kiln Cup pairing; use origin/Edition relevance; no discount assumption; material/ritual plates should return the user to coffee.  
**Must not copy:** Identical row composition, product names, or “perfect pairing” lifestyle categories.  
**Informs:** PDP related products, Kiln Cup pairing, article inline commerce.  
**Viewport:** Desktop; simplify to swipe-free compact grid/list on mobile.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY.

## Canyon — journal and full-bleed editorial

### REF-CANYON-JOURNAL-INDEX-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 122654(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Canyon article/journal index.  
**Observe:** Oversized taxonomy-like title; tiny sort/view controls; three large editorial images; metadata, title, and short excerpt below each; generous top and inter-card space.  
**Why it works:** The index reads as a publication, not a generic blog grid. Imagery and titles establish authorship before utility controls.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Publication opening, dominant featured imagery, concise card metadata, high whitespace, clear image/title links.  
**Red Clay change:** Only three launch Editions exist: one featured and two secondary, no category/filter theater, use volume/folio labels and region connections.  
**Must not copy:** Article taxonomy sentence, three-equal-column structure, imagery, or view controls.  
**Informs:** `/journal`, Edition cards, article masthead.  
**Viewport:** Desktop; stacked featured-first mobile.  
**Weight:** PRIMARY.

### REF-CANYON-LIFESTYLE-FULLBLEED-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 184725(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Full-bleed Canyon lifestyle/product scene.  
**Observe:** Edge-to-edge environmental photography with very small corner caption/CTA; products are present inside a believable domestic setting; UI disappears almost entirely.  
**Why it works:** It creates atmosphere and scale without additional components.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Occasional full-width visual release, tiny publication caption, product-in-ritual context, photography carrying the section.  
**Red Clay change:** Use morning light, terracotta, linen, basalt, clear coffee, and a Red Clay-specific domestic/architectural setting; ensure the coffee remains visible and imagery does not resemble a resort or generic California patio.  
**Must not copy:** Scene, table composition, Canyon products, caption position, or sun-washed grade.  
**Informs:** Homepage hero/Kiln ritual, PDP visual interlude, About closing plate.

**Weight:** PRIMARY.

## Onyx — selective motion and promotional confidence

### REF-ONYX-NAV-PROMO-01

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 194039(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Onyx expanded navigation with search, category list, feature card, and colorful promotional tile wall visible beneath.  
**Observe:** Large overlay command area; confident image/text feature; sharp contrast between navigation and dense product promotion; bolder typographic overlays on imagery.  
**Why it works:** The navigation has high visual confidence and can direct attention to a chosen launch product.  
**Borrow conceptually:** Confidence of one feature moment, decisive menu entry/exit, bolder desktop scale when a promotion genuinely matters.  
**Red Clay change:** Reduce density dramatically; use one origin/Edition plate, mineral surfaces, quiet typography, and no promotional wall.  
**Must not copy:** Search-bar dominance, vertical category volume, overlay typography, pastel product tiles, or ecommerce density.  
**Informs:** Optional future desktop menu feature and high-impact Edition promotion.  
**Viewport:** Desktop.  
**Weight:** SECONDARY.

### REF-ONYX-NAV-PROMO-02

**Screenshot:** `Screenshot 2026-08-21 194049(1).png`  
**Screen/context:** Alternate Onyx expanded-navigation features: physical location, subscriptions, cold coffee.  
**Observe:** Three tall image-led promotional columns, each with overlaid title and dark action label; the menu and campaign tiles behave as one branded field.  
**Why it works:** Strong modular campaign composition and direct visual routing.

## Reference anti-copy checklist

- No Canyon wordmark, product silhouette, packaging graphics, copy, palette, exact spacing, or exact grid recreation.
- No Onyx promo-wall density, maximal type overlays, futuristic coloration, or category overload.
- No customer star ratings from Canyon’s PDP reference.
- No mega-menu solely because references have one.
- No hover behavior without keyboard/touch equivalent and visible essential information.
- No image-generated text; product labels and captions are rendered in code/design.
