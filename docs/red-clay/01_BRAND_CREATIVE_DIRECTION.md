# Red Clay Coffee — Production Creative Direction

**Purpose:** A production-facing distillation of the approved brand world. Later corrected sources govern any conflict with the original strategy.

## Brand thesis

**Red Clay is a contemporary African coffee house built around the material character of place.** It presents East African coffee through confident editorial storytelling, tactile materiality, and quiet domestic ritual. The work must feel culturally grounded and visually authored without pretending the fictional company has real sourcing relationships.

## Positioning

Red Clay sits between clinical specialty-coffee minimalism and vague lifestyle branding. It treats coffee as a living agricultural product worthy of publication-grade presentation while retaining the directness and utility of a strong storefront.

The portfolio proposition is editorial commerce with African specificity: restrained enough to be trusted, tactile enough to be remembered, and usable enough to be purchased from.

## The continuum

| Beat | Production meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Earth** | Landscape, agricultural context, mineral palette, laterite/basalt mass | Do not claim soil mechanically creates a tasting note. |
| **Coffee** | Primary product, sensory character, verified region/process context | Do not reduce coffee to dashboards or fictional traceability data. |
| **Vessel** | The Kiln Cup as one companion object connecting material and brew | Do not let ceramics become a second brand universe. |
| **Ritual** | Morning light, pouring, holding, brewing, and hospitality | Do not drift into generic beige lifestyle imagery. |

## Emotional territory

- Grounded calm
- Architectural substance
- Luminous warmth
- Quiet confidence
- Tactile presence
- Unhurried attention without interaction friction

The site should feel like handling an unusually well-made monograph while remaining an efficient commerce experience.

## Material world

**Foundational:** coffee, earth/landscape, and human agricultural agency.  
**Structural support:** laterite, rammed earth, compressed earthen surfaces, volcanic basalt/obsidian, disciplined rules, weight, shadow.  
**Warmth and ritual:** raw terracotta, bone/mineral-white glaze, unbleached linen, pale timber, steam, clear dark coffee, low morning light.  
**Publication layer:** tactile paper, concise captions, folio numbering, asymmetric image plates, controlled reading measures.

Materials are not decoration. They determine photographic surfaces, color contrast, layout mass, borders/rules, and transition language.

## Coffee-first hierarchy

1. Current coffees and buying clarity.
2. Place, process context, and sensory story.
3. The Editions and cultural/editorial depth.
4. The Kiln Cup and material ritual.
5. Architecture as visual framing.

The hero must identify a coffee house. Products enter the homepage early. The Shop remains direct and filter-free. PDPs allow a buying decision before long-form reading. The Kiln Cup page stays shorter than a coffee PDP and returns users to coffee.

## Cultural representation

- Represent East African people as contemporary, self-possessed subjects with agency, expertise, and dignity.
- Show work as skilled agricultural or quality practice, not anonymous labor spectacle.
- Distinguish Central Kenya, Kayanza/Burundi, and Southern Ethiopia rather than using a flattened pan-African visual shorthand.
- Verify geographic and agricultural terms before publication.
- Generated people must be presented as editorial concept imagery, never as real producers, partners, or interview subjects.
- Avoid poverty framing, charity language, staged gratitude, safari cues, pseudo-ethnic styling, invented motifs, and tourist gaze.

## Voice

**Qualities:** assured, literate, tactile, warm, precise, culturally grounded, calm.  
**Cadence:** short-to-medium editorial sentences with occasional longer descriptive passages; no frantic selling.  
**Balance:** sensory storytelling supported by factual specificity. Facts provide context; they do not become deterministic flavor equations.

Preferred verbs and nouns are concrete: grow, wash, dry, roast, pour, hold, earth, coffee, vessel, ridge, canopy, clay, light. CTAs remain plain: `Explore the Harvest`, `View Coffee`, `Add to Bag`, `Explore Origins`, `Read the Edition`, `Join the Dispatch`.

Avoid exoticizing language, faux-science, inflated luxury vocabulary, “unlock/acquire,” exclamation-heavy promotion, charity claims, and unverified trade language.

## Visual attitude

- **Core — Contemporary Monograph:** spacious editorial typography, image-led chapters, fine rules, publication rhythm.
- **Support — Mineral Architecture:** strong planes, asymmetric grids, weight, shadow, and clipping geometry.
- **Warmth — Luminous Editorial:** direct morning light, skin-tone fidelity, coffee appetite, domestic calm.

Contrast is important: bright bone/linen fields against basalt depth; laterite/terracotta used with purpose rather than bathing the entire site in brown. Photography should retain tactile detail and believable material imperfections.

## Fictional-brand boundary

Allowed when marked and later resolved:

- fictional product and Edition structures;
- provisional sensory directions;
- concept packaging and Kiln Cup imagery;
- verified regional background presented as context.

Not allowed:

- identifiable station/producer relationships, interviews, quotations, contracts, premiums, certifications, impact, or direct-trade claims;
- legacy product names presented as current products;
- final prices, stock, logistics, harvest counts, dispatch claims, or subscription savings without approval;
- Kiln Cup safety, capacity, dimensions, ergonomics, heat retention, care, or durability as proven facts;
- universal `72-hour` processing or any simple soil/elevation/process → flavor claim.

## Rejected tropes and territories

- Mandatory map, topographic interface, elevation simulator, WebGL/Three.js, 3D book, light simulator, scientific flavor dashboard.
- Sterile laboratory minimalism; generic luxury beige; muddy brown monochrome.
- Safari or resort imagery; rustic “mud hut” exoticism; pseudo-ethnic display typography.
- Generic café scenes, laptop lifestyle, glossy plastic packaging, fake generated text.
- Generic bento/cards, glassmorphism, gradients, SaaS hero, icon features, ratings, sales, urgency, metrics, countdowns.
- Animation for its own sake, ubiquitous fade-up, parallax excess, scroll-jacking, decorative particles.

## Responsive personality

### Mobile — pocket monograph

- Complete, linear, legible, touch-first.
- Coffee and primary action appear early.
- Dedicated portrait imagery and safe focal crops.
- Natural scrolling; compact metadata; accessible sheets/drawers.
- Motion is selective, lightweight, and never required.
- The Earthen Folio is a stacked three-chapter sequence.

### Desktop — architectural publication

- Wider asymmetric grids, generous empty space, and large photographic plates.
- Bounded sticky purchase or editorial compositions where useful.
- Hover reveals only supplementary information.
- Motion may differentiate region chapters and material planes, but ordinary navigation and purchasing remain immediate.
- The signature experience creates a clear authored interval, then releases back into calm document flow.

## Production test

At every review, ask:

1. Is coffee still the first product and clearest action?
2. Could this layout belong to any premium coffee template?
3. Is African specificity coming from place, people, material, and editorial accuracy rather than motifs?
4. Does the page remain complete without motion?
5. Has any fictional or provisional detail begun to read as fact?
